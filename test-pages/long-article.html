A plain guide to layer 2

Why layer 2 exists

Layer 2 networks exist because Ethereum mainnet can only process a limited number of transactions per second, which keeps fees high when demand rises.

How rollups work

A rollup executes transactions off the main chain and posts compressed data back to Ethereum. Optimistic rollups assume transactions are valid and allow a challenge window of about seven days.

Zero-knowledge rollups

Zero-knowledge rollups instead post a cryptographic proof that the batch was computed correctly, so withdrawals can finish much faster.

Where the fees go

Fees on a rollup have two parts: the cost of execution on the layer 2, which is tiny, and the cost of posting data to Ethereum, which dominates.

Blobs and Dencun

Since the Dencun upgrade in March 2024, rollups post data in blobs, which cut layer 2 fees by roughly an order of magnitude.

Bridges

Bridges move assets between chains. A canonical bridge is run by the rollup itself; third-party bridges are faster but add trust assumptions.

Sequencers

Sequencers order transactions on most rollups today. Most are run by a single company, which is a centralisation risk the teams plan to remove.

Forced inclusion

If a sequencer goes offline, users can usually force transactions through the contract on Ethereum, though it is slower and costs more.

Choosing one

Choosing a layer 2 depends on what you need: low fees, fast withdrawals, the apps you want to use, or the strength of the security model.

A rule of thumb

For small payments, almost any rollup is fine. For large sums, look at how decentralised the sequencer and the upgrade keys are.
